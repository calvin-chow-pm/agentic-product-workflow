How the team worked at Thinkific

At Thinkific, I built and operated customer-feedback grouping and prioritization, competitive and industry monitoring, sales/support synthesis, and product delivery workflows. Alongside another PM leading AI experimentation, I tested, implemented, and shared these workflows.

Competitive monitoring covered credentialing, pricing, packaging, and monetization, with a daily digest and weekly brief. Specialist context was kept focused instead of giving every agent every memory.

02 / Evaluate

Scored on 4 dimensions against 15 hand-scored examples

I manually scored 15 past competitive insights to establish anchors. Each dimension was scored separately: 0 = fails, 2 = partially meets, 4 = strongly meets. Approval history was a separate routing signal, not an accuracy measure.

Real example: an agent read a competitor’s support-page update as a new, plan-exclusive launch. The page said neither. See the correction ↓

Check changes before relying on them

  1. Reviewed memory updates

    I reviewed consequential updates before applying them to future work.

  2. Historical replay

    I replayed the existing memory queue to compare outputs before and after a consequential update.

  3. One-week parallel comparison

    New insights went through old and new evaluation approaches for a week so I could compare differences.

  4. Source sampling every two weeks

    Every two weeks, I manually sampled sources to check collection, evidence extraction, grouping, and scoring.

04 / Connect to action

Learn from what happened after approval.

When an insight influenced product work, I kept a lightweight decision receipt linking the evidence to the decision, action, and observed outcome.

Agent record

Keep the decision trail together

  1. Evidence

    Sources and the customer or business problem.

  2. Recommendation

    The proposed response and its reasoning.

  3. Human decision

    What I approved, changed, or declined, and why.

  4. Product action

    What was actually implemented.

  5. Observed outcome

    What happened, supported by evidence and its limits.

The receipt feeds relevant outcomes back to the coordinating and specialist agents, so they can learn beyond whether a suggestion was approved.

Supporting example

A real monitoring error and how I corrected it

Reconstructed from a competitor-monitoring incident at Thinkific; wording is paraphrased.

The signal

A competitor changed its certification analytics support page. It described analytics exports across plans, with capabilities depending on plan level, and still did not fully support direct API integration with customers’ custom data warehouses.

The agent’s interpretation

Certification analytics access, exports, and warehouse integrations were all new and exclusive to the competitor’s Plus-equivalent plan.

The correction

The page did not establish that launch or exclusivity. Analytics access, plan-dependent exports, and warehouse integration needed to be evaluated separately, with the integration limitation preserved.

Why it mattered

A relevant competitive signal can still be inaccurate. Checking the source exposed an unsupported premise for a product or packaging response.
